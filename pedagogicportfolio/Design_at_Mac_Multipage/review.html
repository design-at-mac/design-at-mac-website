Developmental review

Structured, interpretive, and respectful

The peer-mentoring process is formative. It helps the writer strengthen reasoning, evidence, reflection, and next steps. It is not a formal promotion or performance decision.

1

Declare purpose

State the review task, materials, relationship, and limitations.

2

Understand context

Summarize the reflection accurately before evaluating it.

3

Apply criteria

Examine learning focus, reasoning, scholarship, and iteration.

4

Offer feedforward

Name strengths, gaps, priority revisions, and the next evidence need.

Peer mentoring

Good conversation surfaces thinking behind the text

Writers and reviewers use questions to uncover values, assumptions, evidence limits, surprises, and future teaching questions.

Writer preparation

  • Identify the central learning problem
  • State the feedback needed
  • Bring evidence and uncertainties

Reviewer preparation

  • Trace the red thread
  • Separate evidence from interpretation
  • Identify one high-value revision priority

Conversation prompts

  • What does the reflection reveal about students?
  • Which assumptions underpin the decisions?
  • What surprised the educator?

Closing the conversation

  • Summarize what was heard
  • Choose revision priorities
  • Identify the next inquiry and evidence plan
