Student perspectives deepen inquiry.

Use feedback, consultation, partnership, co-design, and co-inquiry carefully and ethically.

Evidence and partnership

Student perspectives can enter at several points

The appropriate level of involvement depends on purpose, relationships, ethical considerations, time, and context. Not every reflection needs co-design, but every claim about students should be proportionate to the evidence.

Feedback is a signal, not always an explanation.Follow-up inquiry can clarify what students encountered and why.
FeedbackSurveys, critical incidents, reflections, and comments.
ConsultationInterviews and conversations that explore meaning.
PartnershipJoint interpretation or planning.
Co-designShared development of learning activities or assessment.
Co-inquiryCollaborative investigation of learning and teaching.
Interpretation

Different evidence supports different claims

Confidence, satisfaction, engagement, performance, and learning are related but not interchangeable. Portfolio writers should name exactly what the evidence represents.

Student confidence

Supports claims about perceived confidence or readiness, not mastery by itself.

Student interviews

Illuminate experienced barriers and supports, but may not represent all students.

Student work

Demonstrates assessed performance, but not necessarily why learning changed.

Faculty observation

Provides process insight, but benefits from corroboration and transparent criteria.

Course evaluations

Describe broad perceptions according to the questions asked, not complete effectiveness.

Triangulation

Combines sources to strengthen interpretation without pretending uncertainty disappears.
